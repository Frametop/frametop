# Hand cutouts and near input

These sections were split out of [README.md](README.md) to keep it readable.

## Hand cutouts and near input

ft-screens reads the hands file (version 2's skeleton when it's there, else version 1's points) and the gestures file once per tick (`screens/handcut.cpp`). It cuts only with Room View on: with it off, a hole would show the void or the game, not your hand (see "Rendered hands"). `cutouts state` ends with `room view on|off (why)`; `cutouts roomview auto|on|off` forces it.

**Off by default since 2026-10-09** (`HANDS_CUTOUTS=0`): ft-handview draws the hands over the panels instead (see "Rendered hands"). `HANDS_CUTOUTS=1`, or `ft-handsctl cutouts on` for the session, brings the holes back.

**The matte** (`HANDS_MATTE=circle`, the default since 2026-10-09) is a round hole per hand: a disk facing the eye, centred on the middle of what the head sees of the hand (the box around its joints, each with its half-width, the wrist's 2.5 cm), wide enough to reach the hand's furthest edge from there, plus the margin. Each eye sees the disk on the screen as an ellipse (a circle on a screen square to it), drawn as the stadium around it with the capsules' soft edge (15% of its radius). It hides more of the screen than the hand needs, and with it the hand's tracking error and the few degrees Room View's picture of the hand sits off it, until the tracking is good enough for a hand-shaped hole. The forearm isn't in it. Building and projecting it costs 4 us median on the desk-work replay (the mesh 26 us), the composite the same as the mesh's or less (`scripts/bench-matte.sh` on probe-moves: 2 pieces per eye against 35).

`HANDS_MATTE=mesh` is hand-shaped: a tapered capsule per finger bone, with real fingers' half-widths (10.5 mm at the index's knuckle down to 7.5 mm at its tip, scaled by the hand's palm length); the palm as a 2.4 cm slab through the wrist and the knuckles, widened to the hand's edges; the thumb's web; and an 18 cm stub of the forearm toward the skeleton's elbow (along the hand's axis without a skeleton). The palm, web and forearm are convex pieces, each drawn as its outline grown by the margin with a soft 2 px edge. `HANDS_MATTE=capsules` draws ft-hands' capsules instead, as before, for comparison (`cutouts matte circle|mesh|capsules` switches it live).

Its margin is an angle seen from the eyes, so it covers the same share of the tracking error at any distance: `HANDS_MATTE_MARGIN` (1.5 degrees) with the hand still, plus `HANDS_MATTE_MOTION` (25 ms) of the hand's motion, at most `HANDS_MATTE_MOTION_MAX` (1.5 degrees) more. The cutout placement probes of 2026-10-08 (frame-hands `captures/probe-2026100*/video_analysis`) measured the cutout points against the hand Room View shows: 1.8-2.4 degrees median still (p90 4.1-4.7), 2.3-3.1 moving at 0.1-0.8 m/s, 4-6 above 0.8 m/s. Part of that is the video landmark model's own error, and an edge needs only the error across it (about 70% of a point's). A margin covering the p90 (4.5 degrees, 3 cm at 40 cm) would turn each finger into a 7 cm blob; 1.5 covers about the median. `cutouts margin DEG [MS [MAX]]` changes it live, and so does the Hand cutout margin slider on the Hands page of Frametop Input Settings (0-3 degrees in quarter steps; it sends the still margin only and writes `HANDS_MATTE_MARGIN`). The prediction lead stays 36 ms (`HANDS_MATTE_LEAD`, `cutouts lead`): the probes' best lead for fast hands was 6-14 ms, but 36 costs only 0.1-0.2 degrees.

The probes also saw the hand's picture off by an amount that depended on its distance, but not the same way in any two runs, so the correction is a hook, off: `HANDS_MATTE_SHIFT="0.35:0.5,-0.3 0.6:-1,0"` would turn the matte's hands by those yaw and pitch angles (degrees, right and up positive) at those distances from the head, in between linearly (`cutouts shift ...|off` live).

Cost, offline (`scripts/bench-matte.sh` on the probe-moves and lit replays, a 3440x1935 screen 0.6 m ahead, GPU time to finish each buffer on an otherwise idle GPU): the mesh 3.9-4.1 ms per full buffer against 3.8-3.9 for the capsules, and 3.4-3.6 against 3.0-3.4 when only the hands' boxes are redrawn (the margin is wider than the capsules' 4 mm). CPU 0.2 ms per composite either way; building and projecting the matte 14-24 us median.

**Near input** (`screens/handinput.cpp`, frame-hands `notes/hand-interaction.md` sections 3, 5, 16, 17, 18 and 19): a bare hand whose index tip, thumb-index contact, knuckles or palm is over a panel (or within the grab band outside it) and within `HANDS_NEAR_REACH` (0.075 m, about 3 inches; 0.30 on 2026-10-10 before the live test, 0.15 before that) in front of it touches it: ft-screens tells the pointer helper so ("near ... 1"), which then hides that hand's ray and leaves its pinches and grips to ft-screens. Beyond it a pinch is the ray's or the gaze's. A hand stays near until it's 2.5 cm farther out, then 200 ms on, so the ray doesn't flicker at the edge; in that margin the ray stays hidden ("near ... 2") but the hand's pinches are the helper's again. The ray shows again once its pointing pose has held 120 ms, about 0.35 s after the fingertip passes 10 cm. A hand resting low by the body (the poke's resting rule below: typing, the lap) touches only within 4 cm, so a low panel within reach doesn't take its gaze pinches. While a hand is within 0.30 m of a panel, ft-screens asks ft-hands for its full rate (want-hz), so a tap from outside the zone is seen at 30 Hz. Near a panel:
- **Poke to click:** the index fingertip presses when it comes at the panel's face (user, 2026-10-08): past the surface for two hands frames in a row, or for one when it came in decisively (1.7 cm in on average over the last 0.3 s, on its way in on the frame before too, at 0.1 m/s or more, and under 2.5 cm in coming nearer on each of the last 3 frames: `HANDS_POKE_TRAVEL`, 0 = always two frames; 2.5 cm until the second live test of 2026-10-10, which refused taps that came 1.7 and 2.0 cm). A quick tap at 30 Hz is past the surface for one or two frames, and one the tracker has a centimetre short for none: two frames in a row lost most shallow taps in the live test of 2026-10-10. It also needs: seen by two cameras, moving into the surface at 4 cm/s or more when it crossed (over the last 100 ms), the finger (knuckle to tip) within `HANDS_POKE_ANGLE` (50) degrees of the panel's normal, the index straight (1.5) and either no other fingertip more than 6 mm deeper than its tip or the hand pointing (the index straighter than each other finger by 0.2; until the second live test the index had to be 5 mm nearer than the others, and the tracker often reads a pointing hand's middle finger nearly as straight, its tip within -7 to +4 mm of the index tip's depth: 14 of its 39 refusals), not past the surface for more than 5 frames or 4 cm (reaching through), and not from a hand resting low by the body (palm more than `HANDS_POKE_REST_BELOW`, 0.30 m, under the eyes and within `HANDS_POKE_REST_NEAR`, 0.32 m, of the head across the floor; it stays resting until 5 cm higher or further out). So typing under a low panel doesn't poke it, and reaching out to one does. It lets go 1.5 cm out, or 1 cm back from the deepest point (so a tracker that has the finger behind the panel still lets go). The click stays where it was pressed until the fingertip slides 1.5 cm; then it drags. No new press for 150 ms after a release, nor until the tip has been 1 cm out (a noisy pull-back pressed twice); a finger 8 cm through the panel lets go and needs to come back out first. Within 5 cm the pointer follows the fingertip. A tap up to 1.5 cm past the content's edge presses at the edge (the desktop's taskbar is on the bottom edge). The curve, reset, dock and close buttons take pokes too (below: Buttons). The index crossing the plane farther off the content (a hand reaching for the bar or a corner) isn't a poke: ft-screens logs it once, as "not a poke", with where it crossed. On synthetic taps with 3-6 mm of tracking noise, 50-56 of 60 shallow taps (0.5 cm past the surface) press, against 34-36 with the two-frame rule; a finger held still 1.5-2.5 cm out presses no more often than before (`handinput-test`, TestPokeNoisy).
- **The hand owns the pointer:** while a hand holds the button, SteamVR's laser moves (the pointer helper's head or gaze pointer, a controller) go nowhere, and on a panel whose pointer a fingertip moved in the last 250 ms neither do they. Before, the helper's pointer a few cm off the fingertip pulled the remote host's pointer away between the poke's press and release: a drag, not a click.
- **Why a poke didn't press:** ft-screens' log has a line for each press and release ("hands: right poke pressed screen 104 at 1932,869 (-0.6 cm, in at 0.25 m/s from 3.1 cm out, 1 frame(s) past, the finger 12 deg off)"), and for each time the fingertip crossed a panel's surface without a press, the rules that held it back ("hands: right poke on screen 104 didn't press (0.4 cm deep at most): past the surface for 1 frame(s); ..."). `handinput state` counts them.
- **Pinch to move or resize:** a pinch where the thumb and index meet (not the gestures file's point, the knuckles, 5-8 cm behind them) within the border band takes the panel: 6 cm outside its edges to 2.5 cm inside (`HANDS_GRAB_BAND`), anywhere in the touch zone's depth (in front of it, or a little behind), or up to 3 cm beyond it when the touch zone has the hand (the third live test: "the fingers +9.0 cm from it, out of the touch zone: nothing", the pointer helper having left the pinch to ft-screens). By any corner (6 cm) it resizes, and the opposite corner stays put: a screen or remote display along its diagonal (its aspect is its resolution's), a floating window in pixels from that corner (every corner of a window since 2026-10-10, its title bar's ends too). Anywhere else on the border, the bar, a floating window's title bar, it moves. Moved, it follows the knuckles 1:1 (closer and farther too) and keeps facing you. A soft white glow shows where a pinch would take it (below: Glows at corners, edges and buttons); with `HANDS_EDGE_GLOW=0` the resize handle goes to the corner a pinch is near instead, brighter the nearer, lit blue while resizing. With `HANDS_CUTOUTS=1` a glow on the panel also shows what a pinch would do there: amber at the corner (resize), blue on the border (move), green for a card pinch; a dot while it holds. (The glows are drawn into the cutout buffer, so with the cutouts off, the default since the drawn hands, they don't show.)
- **Resize from any corner, by hand, gaze or laser** (2026-10-10, as on a Vision Pro): each panel has one resize handle, and it goes to the corner something aims at: a laser's ray (a controller's, or the pointer helper's, which the gaze and the hand ray drive) landing within a fifth of the panel's height (5-20 cm) of a corner (or a hand's pinch at one, with `HANDS_EDGE_GLOW=0`; hands have their own glow since the fourth live test). Since 2026-10-13 (user: "remove the radio icon thing on corners for resizing and replace them with the glow that is used for grabbing with hand tracking") the handle draws nothing: the same soft white L round the corner as a hand's pinch gets shows it, brighter as the aim comes to the corner, brightest while resizing (the handle is still there, just outside the corner, to take the laser's press); with no aim for 0.4 s it goes back to the bottom right. Press it with the laser, or look at it and pinch (a gaze pinch on a resize handle or a grab bar is a press after 0.12 s held still, `POINTER_GAZE_CONTROL_HOLD`, not the 0.5 s elsewhere), and drag: the corner follows, the opposite one stays put.
- **Pinch on the content to click** (`HANDS_PINCH_CLICK`, 2026-10-10): a pinch within the touch zone, not in the band, presses where the fingertip's pointer was (if it was there in the last half second, within 4 cm of the fingers), else where the fingers meet; it's held while the pinch is, and moving the pinched hand 1.5 cm drags. Not from a hand resting low by the body or seen by one camera.
- **Card pinch** (`HANDS_CARD_PINCH`, 2026-10-10: "pinch from underneath the display and manipulate it like a card"): a pinch closing on the panel's edge with the panel between the thumb and the index (each tip at least 3 mm from its plane on some frame in the last 0.3 s, the line between them within 60 degrees of its normal, the hand along the panel: at least 45 degrees off its normal, the fingers within 5 cm of its border), and the index and middle fingers together against the thumb (thumb to middle tip within 4.5 cm, index to middle tip within 3.5 cm) or all four fingertips bunched at it (each within 5 cm: the "pinched fingers" hand), holds it like a card (since the second live test; the index alone was enough before, and is now the border's move or a corner's resize; the middle finger joining within 0.3 s of the pinch makes the move a card from there): it goes rigidly with the hand, its place and its turn about all three axes, 1:1, smoothed, past 1 cm and 3 degrees; no facing you, no level snap. An edge pinch from the front or the side has both tips on one side of the plane, or the hand pointing into it: that's the border's move.
- **Fist grab** (`HANDS_FIST_GRAB`, 2026-10-10: "use the fist to expand and rotate the display, as well as change its angle"): a grip (ft-hands' closed hand) made with the palm, knuckles or curled middle finger within the touch zone over a panel (or within 6 cm of its edge) takes it; so does a pinch by a closed hand (the index 1.15 extended or less, the other three 1.05 or less on average: ft-hands calls a fist with the thumb on the index a pinch, and in the third live test two fists clicked the content instead of grabbing it). It's held while either the grip or that pinch is, until the hand opens: the fist carries it 1:1 and turning the fist turns it about all three axes, as the wrist turns a pinched one (below); open the hand to let go. Two fists (or a fist and a pinch) on one panel stretch it and turn it, as two pinches do. Since the fourth live test (2026-10-10, "I can't grab using fists"): a fist is at a panel with its palm, knuckles or curled middle finger up to `HANDS_FIST_REACH` (0.11 m) in front of it or 10 cm behind it (a live fist went 8.5 cm through and wasn't "at it"), and a hand curled that far (the three fingers under 1.2) is ft-screens' that far out, so the pointer helper leaves its grip; a pinch on the content by a hand that's closing (the three fingers under 1.4 and down by 0.1 in the last 150 ms: a fist's thumb meets the index first) waits up to 150 ms to become a fist (then a fist grab) before it clicks, where it began. A fist farther away is the pointer helper's grip (a gaze drag). A fist or a pinch by a hand "grab that" has (held out open, or closing into its fist, aiming at a panel it would take) waits until that gesture fires, and then does nothing, or lets the hand go, at most 0.6 s, and then it's what the hand is by then (a fist, a second anchor, or a pinch): an open hand held out to a panel then a fist pulled back and turned over is "grab that", a fist made at the panel without that is a fist grab. At a panel within arm's reach "grab that" doesn't aim, so nothing waits.
- **Turn it with the wrist** (2026-10-10): while one hand carries a panel (by its border or bar, or a fist), turning the wrist turns the panel the same way, 1:1, about all three axes (yaw, pitch, roll), to line displays up. The hand's turn is read from its wrist and index and little knuckles (they hold still while the fingers pinch), smoothed (One Euro, 1 Hz still), relative to facing your head across the floor from the hand, and applied on top of the panel facing you: carrying a panel round you still only keeps it facing you, and a wrist turn adds to that. Nothing turns until the hand has turned `HANDS_GRAB_ROTATE_DEADZONE` (5 degrees; the hand frame jitters about 1 degree a frame and drifts up to 6 over a second and a half held still), then it turns from there without a jump. Within 2.5 degrees of level it snaps level. Let go and it keeps the turn; the next grab starts from it. Corner and tab grabs only resize (letting go with one of two hands, the other's turn starts afresh). `HANDS_GRAB_ROTATE=0` keeps the old facing-only carry. A panel held near the eyes by a point far from its centre (closer than twice that distance) faces you from a point between the hand and its centre: facing from the centre turned it about the hand, which moved the centre, which turned it, and 0.6 m panels held by an edge at arm's reach spun and flipped between frames on the third live test's replays.
- **Two hands:** both hands holding one panel (pinches or fists) stretch it (its width follows their distance, past a 1 cm dead zone) and turn it with the line between them on every axis: swing the pair round (yaw), raise one hand (roll), bring one nearer, and tip both wrists the same way about the line between them to pitch it (the mean of the two wrists' turns about that line, past `HANDS_GRAB_ROTATE_DEADZONE`); it snaps level within 2.5 degrees. `HANDS_STRETCH_TURN=0`: about the vertical only (before 2026-10-10's third live test). Let go with one and the other carries on. The second hand doesn't have to find a grab zone (since the fourth live test, where it clicked the content instead): while one hand holds a panel, the other's pinch or fist anywhere on it, up to `HANDS_JOIN_REACH` (0.15 m) in front of it, 12 cm behind or 10 cm round it, joins as the second anchor; it never clicks, pokes, pushes or flicks a panel the other hand holds. Replaying the user's two-fist flags (215945 and 215955) with the panel where the live log had it, and 4 cm nearer or 4-8 cm farther: before, a click and no two-hand hold; now both hands hold it 9.4 s, stretching and turning it. A carried panel (one hand or two) turns at most 6 degrees a tick about the hand's point (a fist pulled in toward the face flipped it between ticks in a replay).
- **Holding through tracking loss** (2026-10-10: losing the hand dropped what it held): a pinch, card or fist grab, and a poke's or pinch's press, last until a clear release: the hand seen with its pinch or grip up and its fingers open (thumb to index tip over 3.5 cm; a fist's fingers over 1.35 extended) on 2 hands frames in a row (67 ms), or up for 6 frames whatever the fingers say. A pinch that flickers up for a frame or two in fast motion, or that ft-hands ended as lost while the hand comes back still pinched, holds on. Unseen for `HANDS_HOLD_LOST_MS` (1.75 s), it lets go where it is. Meanwhile the panel coasts on the hand's last velocity, slowing to a stop over 150 ms; a frame whose point jumped more than 12 cm counts as unseen (up to 2 in a row). When the hand is back, the smoothing restarts moving at the gap's average velocity and the panel blends to the hand over 100 ms (longer when that's more than 0.6 m/s on average). Offline, carries at 0.6 m/s losing the hand for 3, 6 and 10 frames hold, the panel's largest step 12-17 mm a 90 Hz tick (6.7 moving). A hold also lasts while the pointer helper's word says that hand is "off" (not a controller's) for up to `HANDS_HOLD_LOST_MS`: the word flickered off 24 times in the third live test's 7 minutes and dropped three holds.
- **Tracking no hand makes** (`HANDS_TRACK_GATE`, 2026-10-10: "grabbing the side of the monitor, my hand moves off to the left and tracking gets lost: the grabbed monitor is jittery"): on that flag the hand, 45-60 degrees off the head's forward, read alternately at two places 10-20 cm apart and its frame flipped 100-180 degrees between frames. A holding hand's frame that moves faster than 4 m/s from the last one kept, or lands more than 0.5 x 100 m/s² x dt² + 2 cm from where its velocity had it, is dropped (three in a row at one new place: it moved there, and the panel blends over); its frame turning faster than 540 degrees a second likewise. Frames seen by one camera, or 50-80 degrees off the head's forward, are smoothed harder (the cutoffs down to 0.35 of theirs) and don't turn the panel; past 80 degrees the hand counts as unseen, so a panel carried out of view at the side holds still until it's back. The turn's smoothing follows at most 4 Hz. On the third live test's ten flags, each pinch or fist held 0.4 s or more replayed against a 0.6 m panel at the hand (`handinput-test --carries`): the carried panel's centre moved with a per-tick second difference of 159 mm rms (99th percentile 898) on hands-throw, 9.7 mm (29) now; its turn 33 degrees rms (187) then, 1.6 (5.2) now; the side-grab flag alone 96 mm (599) to 6.2 (23). All 23 flags: 119 mm (719) to 7.6 (26), 26 degrees (157) to 1.3 (4.8).
- **Flick it away** (`HANDS_FLICK`, 2026-10-10): with the hand at a panel (its palm within 12 cm in front of it, over it, nearly still), flick it out fast with the fingers opening: away from you (within 40 degrees) at `HANDS_FLICK_SPEED` (1.2 m/s) or more, 12 cm or more within 0.2 s, the fingers ending open and opening by `HANDS_FLICK_OPENING` (0.15 of mean extension; 0 lets an open hand pushed out flick too). The panel goes out along the line from your eyes through it, to `HANDS_THROW_DISTANCE` (3 m) from them, facing you, over 0.4 s easing out. A pointing hand doesn't flick (its fingers read about 1.3), and an open hand coming through a panel faster than 0.8 m/s doesn't poke it. On the recordings, with a panel 0.3-0.45 m ahead, once in 11 minutes (6 times without the opening rule); none in the 13 "grab that" flags.
- **Throw it** (`HANDS_THROW`, 2026-10-10): let go of a panel you carry with one hand (a card, a fist, a pinch on its border, bar or title; not a resize or two hands) while the hand moves away from you (within 60 degrees) at `HANDS_THROW_SPEED` (1.0 m/s) or more, over the 0.1 s before the fingers opened, and it flies on that way to 3 m from your eyes, facing you. Let go slower and it stays. "Grab that" brings a thrown or flicked panel back, and the gesture again sends it back out there.
- **Push it away** (`HANDS_PUSH`, 2026-10-10: "pushing against the monitor or window does not make it go away"): an open hand (the four fingers 1.6 extended or more on average, the middle, ring and little too), palm to the panel (within 50 degrees), over it, pushed straight into it (within 30 degrees of its normal) from in front of it or resting on it: at `HANDS_PUSH_SPEED` (0.5 m/s) or more and 5 cm or more in, or slowly 15 cm past its surface. It goes 3 m out like a flick. A pointing index pokes; a flat open hand (1.8 or more) or an open one coming in at the push's speed doesn't. Not from a hand resting low, typing, pinching or gripping, nor within 0.6 s of a hold or a press. On the recordings with a panel 0.3-0.45 m ahead: probe-moves (open hands pushed and pulled to probe the ray's reach) 2-15 pushes in 3.5 minutes, the 2026-09-29 recording none, lit 1; on 23 flags with a panel at arm's reach, one (both hands pushed out 11-19 cm through it at 0.5-0.6 m/s); "grab that"'s open hand held out to a panel at arm's reach (5-10 cm in at 0.14-0.43 m/s) doesn't push.
- **Sent away, a panel keeps its size** (`HANDS_SEND_AWAY_WIDTH`, user, 2026-10-10: "windows should not resize to be smaller when they're sent back"): thrown, flicked, pushed or sent back by "grab that", it keeps its width; one "grab that" shrank to bring it to arm's reach (screens wider than `HANDS_GRAB_THAT_WIDTH`) and still that wide gets the width it had back (`restore`, the default; `keep`: never). In the third live test the 2.5 m desktop came at 0.6 m and was thrown 3 m out at 0.6.
- **The target's glow** (`HANDS_TARGET_GLOW`, user, 2026-10-10: "a glow over the window that grab that is targeting"; after the fourth live test: "too powerful. It should be a white glow and far more subtle. It should only be around the outside of the window"): while an open hand is held out to a panel for "grab that", a soft white halo shows outside that panel's edges, about 1.3 degrees wide (1 cm at arm's reach), at most half opaque at the edge and fading out, nothing over the content; it fades in as the gesture arms (0.2 s) and goes as it fires or is let go. An open palm close in front of a panel, toward it, shows the same halo dimmer (a push or a flick would throw it). One overlay (`frametop.guide.target`) sized and curved to the panel, in its plane, so it shows with the cutouts off.
- **Buttons** (the fourth live test: "it's extremely difficult to interact with the button to make the curve curve and not"): at arm's reach the curve button is 8 mm across, 3.5 mm past the bar's end, and the bar (first in the list, with a 1 cm margin) took the six pokes the log has at it, 3-5 mm left of its centre. Now the nearest control wins, the round buttons (curve, reset, pin, dock, close) take a fingertip or a pinch at least `HANDS_BUTTON_REACH` (0.015 m) each way from their centre, and a pinch there presses the button (`HANDS_PINCH_BUTTONS`; 0: the bar's grab zone takes it, as before). A hand at one lights it and shows a soft ring round it. The log names the control pressed.
- **Glows at corners, edges and buttons** (`HANDS_EDGE_GLOW`, user, after the fourth live test: "almost like a cutout of the soft glow we had for highlighting, just around the edges and corners when you're within the interaction zone"): as a hand comes to a panel (in its touch zone), the same soft white halo shows only where a pinch would take it: an L round the corner it would resize from (along both edges from it), a short segment along the edge it would move the panel by, or a ring round a button; brighter as the fingers come closer, brightest while resizing from that corner or pressing that button. One overlay per hand (`frametop.guide.hand.left`, `.right`), so it shows with the cutouts off. With it the resize handle no longer follows hands (lasers and the gaze keep it); `HANDS_EDGE_GLOW=0` brings back the handle at the hand's corner and the cutout buffer's rings.
- **Remote displays** take all of it as screens do: pokes, pinches, the card and fist, corner resize, throws and "grab that".
- **SteamVR's own panels** (2026-10-13, user: "work on the native SteamVR overlay windows"; `screens/vr.cpp`'s "SteamVR's panels by hand", `screens/steampanels.cpp`): the dashboard, the Steam menu, Steam's keyboard and other apps' overlays take a poke or a pinch on the content as a click. Pinches on the border, at a corner or like a card do nothing there and show no glow (user, 2026-10-13: out of scope for SteamVR's panels; handinput's `GrabZone` gives them none). In the headset tests they never moved one, with the pointer's device as the right hand or as a treadmill. The code for those grabs, described below, stays in `screens/vr.cpp` for a later try. The hand ray's pinch on a floating window's grab bar still moves it, like the 3D mouse's laser. ft-screens can't click them itself, so the pointer helper's laser does what the hand does ("touch" lines, the helper's "Touch"): it goes 10 cm in front of the point, aimed in, and presses there. OpenVR doesn't say where a dashboard panel is or how big, so for each hands frame ft-screens casts 7 x 7 rays from the eyes past each index tip (12 degrees each way), keeps the hits within 40 cm of the tip, and fits the panel to their texture coordinates; the overlay keys come from the helper's overlay list ("steamvr"), which it keeps while hands are allowed. When grabs were on, a move or resize first hovered the laser just inside the edge: a floating panel then showed its window controls (`valve.steam.gamepadui.floatingfooter`) under it, and only then did the laser press its grab bar (7.5 cm under the bottom edge) or its resize margin (1 cm past the corner) and follow the hand. A docked dashboard or another app's overlay showed none: the pinch did nothing, and ft-screens' log said so ("no window controls under it"). No fist grabs, two-hand stretch, throws, flicks, pushes, rewinds or two-finger scroll there. One hand at a time: the laser is one device.
- **Smoothing:** a grab carries the knuckles as the cameras saw them, smoothed (`include/fh_smooth.h`: One Euro in continuous time, read each frame ft-screens draws), not the point moved ahead along the hand's velocity as the cutouts use: that velocity is mostly noise times the lead. It moves the panel only once it's 1 cm from where the pinch began, then carries on from there (no jump), so a still hand leaves the panel still. The head the panel turns to face is smoothed too. `HANDS_GRAB_SMOOTH_MINCUTOFF` (1 Hz), `HANDS_GRAB_SMOOTH_BETA` (15 Hz per m/s), `HANDS_GRAB_DEADZONE` (0.01 m). On the probe-moves replay (`scripts/test-handinput.sh`, 90 Hz ticks), the carried point's per-tick second difference went from 13-17 mm rms (50 mm p99) to 2.3-2.6 mm (11 mm), its still wobble from 1.3-1.5 to 0.8-1.0 mm, and it trails the capture by 85 ms while moving (it was 10 ms ahead, by the prediction, and 18 mm off any delay).

**The palm menu** (`screens/palmmenu.cpp`, frame-hands `notes/hand-interaction.md` sections 11 and 18), like the Vision Pro's home indicator: turn a bare hand palm up toward your face, open, and look at it, still. After 0.3 s a small ring (2.5 cm) shows 4 cm above the palm and follows it. A plain thumb-index pinch tap by that hand opens the Steam menu (`steam/ft-steam menu`, what the input relay's `steam_menu` runs), or closes the SteamVR dashboard if it's open. The ring turns blue while the pinch is held, and the release does it; a pinch that's lost, or let go after the hand turned over, does nothing. Turn the hand away for 0.15 s and the ring goes.
- Palm up means: the palm's normal within 45 degrees of the line to your head (it leaves past 60; 60 and 75 before the third live test), the palm within 45 degrees of where your head points (leaves past 55), 15-70 cm from the head, the middle, ring and little fingers open (tip-to-wrist over knuckle-to-wrist 1.5 on average, leaves under 1.3), and the palm moving under 0.25 m/s. A palm up in your lap while you look at a screen doesn't count. The hand ray needs the palm turned away, so the two never overlap.
- Not by a panel, nor busy (the third live test: the Steam menu opened as the user took a panel's bottom edge from below, palm up, fingers bunched): no ring while the hand is near a panel (in its touch zone or margin, or its fingertip, fingers or palm within 12 cm in front of one and 8 cm round it), while near input presses or holds with it or "grab that" has it, nor for a second after any of those. The pinch must be a plain thumb-index one by a still hand: the middle finger out of it (its tip 4.5 cm or more from the thumb's, the finger 1.35 extended or straighter); a card's index and middle or the bunched fingertips do nothing on the ring.
- While the ring shows, ft-screens reports that hand near ("near"), so the pointer helper leaves its pinches alone (no gaze click), and near input leaves it too. A pinch that began before the ring showed stays the helper's.
- Where: a bare hand (not one holding a controller), with hand input allowed: on the desktop, and during a VR game while its dashboard is open or Frametop's panels are turned back on. So in a game it closes the Steam menu it opened; with the dashboard closed, the game keeps your hands. `HANDS_PALM_MENU` (1): 0 turns it off; `ft-screens` command `palmmenu on|off|state`.

**Grab that** (`screens/grabthat.cpp`, 2026-10-10): hold an open hand out toward a screen, a remote display or a floating window, palm to it, then pull the arm back while making a fist, and the panel comes to arm's reach over a quarter of a second: 45 cm ahead of your eyes (`HANDS_GRAB_THAT_DISTANCE`), at the fist's height (at most 45 cm under the eyes), facing you, so the hands can poke it, carry it and turn it. A screen wider than `HANDS_GRAB_THAT_WIDTH` (0.6 m; 0 keeps the size) comes at that width; floating windows keep their size. It doesn't take a panel within arm's reach (below: Which panel); with `HANDS_GRAB_THAT_FROM=0`, as before the fourth live test, the same gesture at a panel that came that way and is still within arm's reach (its centre within 0.8 m of the eyes, wherever you moved it about there) sends it back where it was (and its width), and at a panel within arm's reach that didn't come that way, it centres it at arm's reach. One at a time: a panel that came and is still where it came goes back as another one comes, so they don't stack up (three remote displays did in the live test of 2026-10-10). The reset button sends floating windows back too (the layout reset puts the screens and remote displays back itself). With the panel that near, the open hand held out to it is in its touch zone: its fingers pointing up don't poke it, and its fist or a pinch as it closes waits on the gesture (above).
