# Hand tracking: the skeleton, pinches and flags

These sections were split out of [README.md](README.md) to keep it readable.

## Skeleton

After the 3D step, ft-hands fits a skeleton with your own bone lengths to each hand (`track/skeleton.h`), so fingers keep their length and don't wobble. It's the base for a hand mesh, colliders and the hand ray. `--skeleton 0` (`HANDS_SKELETON=0`) turns it off.

- **Bone lengths, per side.** From clean stereo frames (residual under 4 mm, the model sure of the side), each of 30 numbers is learned: the thumb's CMC and the four knuckles' places in the palm, and the 15 finger bones. A landmark whose two rays miss each other by more than 4 mm, a finger bent more than 90 degrees, or a bone far off the average hand's proportions (under 0.7 or over 1.5 times them) gives no sample. Each settles on the median of its first 45 samples (3 s of a clean hand at 15 Hz), then moves at most 0.02 mm a sample. Until then the estimate is pulled toward a prior: the other side's lengths if it has more, else MediaPipe's average hand times `Hand::scale`. Nothing is saved: each start learns again.
- **The fit, every frame.** The palm is rigid: its frame from the wrist and knuckles, then Gauss-Newton on its rotation and position. Then the thumb and each finger as a chain from its base: the thumb's CMC turns three ways (±50 degrees from a rest pose, ±35 twist), its MCP bends and spreads a little, its IP is a hinge; a finger's knuckle bends (-35 to 95 degrees), spreads (±25, the little finger ±30) and twists a little (±25), the two joints above it are hinges (-10 to 110, -20 to 90). A chain's base may sit up to 15 mm off the rigid palm, pulled back with a 4 mm prior: the measured knuckles wander far more than a palm bends (the little finger's by 3 cm across the palm). Each chain runs 4 Gauss-Newton steps (the limits as clamps) twice, from the measured bone directions and from last frame's pose, and keeps the better fit, so it doesn't hop between two poses that fit about as well.
- **Weights.** Stereo: each landmark by its own ray miss (2 mm at least), and 3.5 times less sure along the line of sight. One view: 3 mm across, 15 mm along it.
- **Which side.** The model's, but a tracked hand keeps its side while the model's running call is between 0.25 and 0.75. The mirror image is tried when the fit is worse than 15 mm rms, and taken after it fits at least twice as well 3 frames in a row; it then sticks while the hand is tracked (a finger can't bend backward, so a hand fitted as the wrong side fits badly once its fingers bend). Switching restarts the filter.
- **Filtering the pose, not the points.** The palm centre and the palm's rotation go through One Euro filters (2 Hz still, plus 30 Hz per m/s and 10 Hz per rad/s); each finger's angles and base through one whose cutoff follows that finger's own speed (2 Hz plus 3 Hz per rad/s), so a pinch on a still palm doesn't lag the way the One Euro on the points does. A chain that fits its landmarks worse than 15 mm this frame moves its filter less (by the square of 15 mm over its error): that's mostly a hidden landmark misplaced.
- **Elbow.** frame-hands' arm model (`notes/forearm.md`): a neck pivot fixed in the head frame, shoulders 18 cm to each side and 6 cm down, upper arm 1.5 times the forearm, the forearm 2.8 times the palm (wrist to middle knuckle), the elbow on the circle the two bones allow, turned 3 degrees from straight down, and never more than 75 degrees off the hand's own axis. ft-hands has no HMD pose, so "down" is the head frame's -y: published with `FH_FIT_ELBOW_HEAD_UP`. The model's other parameters (neck and shoulder offsets) are its starting values: the fitted ones are on the training PC.
- **Published** in the hands file's version 2 (`include/fh_hands.h`): after version 1's struct, which is unchanged, an `fh_hand_fit_t` per hand with the 21 joints (position, a rough radius, and a quaternion in OpenXR's hand-joint axes), the bone lengths, the joint angles, the elbow, the pinch point (between the index and middle knuckles) and the thumb-index contact point, the thumb-index and thumb-middle distances, the fit's rms and how settled the calibration is, and the palm's velocity and angular velocity. Those two are in the head frame and include the head's own motion; for motion in the room, difference room-frame points across publishes as `screens/handcut.cpp` does. Version 1's points and capsules are the skeleton's too, so ft-screens' cutouts get it without a change. The hand recorder's reader takes version 1 or 2.
- The gestures don't use it: pinches still measure the model's world landmarks, and the pinch point and palm-down reading still come from the filtered landmarks. `ft-handreplay --pinch-fitted` counts pinches on the fitted tips for comparison: they begin about as often as on the triangulated tips, more than on the world landmarks.

- **Pinches** don't change: the same counts in every replay, because they don't use the skeleton. On the fitted tips they'd begin more often (lit: 77 against 51; dim: 50 against 35), which is why they stay on the world landmarks.

No elbow labels are here to say how right it is.

## Pinch

ft-hands detects a pinch per hand (`track/pinch.h`) and publishes it to the gestures file. The layout, and how to read it without missing quick taps, is in `include/fh_gestures.h`.

- A pinch begins when the thumb and index tips come within `--pinch-begin` (default 0.020 m). It ends when they open past `--pinch-end` (0.045 m) for 2 processed frames in a row, or when the hand stays lost for 0.25 s (flagged lost).
- A pinch held 0.3 s or longer is a drag, and holds on harder: it ends only once the tips are 5 cm apart (for the same 2 frames), or 0.12 s after they first went past 3.5 cm unless they closed back under 3 cm meanwhile. In the 2026-10-09 headset test drags ended with the tips 3.5-4.0 cm apart, a thumb hidden for a frame or fingers loosened a little mid-drag. On the lit recording's replayed distances, pinches held 0.3 s or more (typing among them) ended 33-134 ms later than before, 200 at most, and one of 25 ran into the next pinch's begin. A grip ends a pinch (as lost) only within 0.3 s of its begin, when closing the hand passes through a pinch; after that no grip begins on that hand, so curling the other fingers around a held pinch doesn't drop it (3 of 28 pinches in the same test). `tests/pinch_test.cpp` (`make check`).
- The distance comes from MediaPipe's world landmarks: the model's own 3D hand pose, averaged over the hand's views, at the user's hand size. `--pinch-triangulated` uses the triangulated tips instead. On two recordings without deliberate pinches, the world landmarks came under 2 cm in 0.2-1% of frames, against 3.3-4.5% for the triangulated tips. In the dim recording, typing still gave 2 pinches a minute (see the next point).
- `--pinch-palm-down MAX` holds back pinches begun with the palm facing down (MAX is the palm normal's share of the head's up axis). The default, 1, turns it off. A close held back that way has to open again before a pinch can begin. Typing curls the thumb onto the index: in the lit recording of 2026-09-30, typing on a keyboard in the lap began 23 pinches in about 2 minutes, all with the palm facing down (0.69-1.00), while the 26 deliberate ones read 0.00-0.50. But in the headset, deliberate pinches with the hand raised in front read 0.90-0.99 too, so the limit is off. Typing is caught by the pointer helper instead: the input relay tells it when you press a key, and the hands on the keys are held back (`POINTER_PINCH_TYPING` and the settings after it, below).
- A hand a pinch is down on stays with that side until the pinch ends. The left/right call is a running average of the model's, and when it flipped mid-pinch, the other side took the same hand and both sides pinched at once.
- The pinch point is between the index and middle knuckles, which hold still while the fingers open and close. The tips' midpoint moved 1-2 cm as a pinch opened, which dragged every release off its press. A drag is the pinch point now, minus where it was when the pinch began, both turned into the room with the HMD pose at their capture times.
- `tools/watch_gestures.py` prints begins, ends and drag offsets live, and `--distance` prints each hand's distance.

## Pinches and grips in the pointer

With `POINTER_HANDS=1`, the pointer helper (`pointer/helper/ft-pointer.cpp`) reads the gestures file every frame. It's off by default.

**The hand ray** (`POINTER_HAND_RAY=1`, the default with `POINTER_HANDS`): a bare hand in front and pointing, palm away, high or low, out in front or lowered toward you (not while it types: the typing guard; `POINTER_RAY_RAISED=30` brings back the old height limit, the knuckles within 30 cm under the eyes, and `POINTER_RAY_FORWARD=25` the old reach limit, at least 25 cm in front of them), shows a ray from it, Quest-style (`include/fh_ray.h`: from a shoulder estimated from the head, through the knuckles), and the ray drives the pointer's virtual controller, so it works on SteamVR's dashboard, Steam's pages, overlays and Frametop's panels like a controller's laser. The ray comes up as the thumb nears the index or middle tip (`POINTER_RAY_READY`). With the ray up, a pinch clicks (a held pinch drags), a middle pinch right-clicks (held and moved, it scrolls), a three-finger pinch (thumb, index and middle together) scrolls as the hand moves, and while a pinch carries a panel, reaching out pushes it away and pulling in brings it near. With the hand low or the palm toward you, there's no ray, and pinches and grips work as below. In gaze mode there's no ray at all unless `POINTER_GAZE_HAND_RAY=1`. A hand holding a controller, or whose controller was used in the last seconds, has no ray and no gestures (the helper's handoff, `pointer/helper/handoff.h`). The rules, timings and measurements are at the top of `ft-pointer.cpp` ("Handoff", "Hand ray") and in frame-hands' `notes/hand-interaction.md`. `pointer/tools/rayeval` runs the ray over a replay (`ft-handreplay --ray`).

- **Pinch to click, in gaze mode.** Your eyes aim and your hands click, Vision Pro style, and the hand ray stays off (`POINTER_GAZE_HAND_RAY`). A pinch locks the pointer where you were looking just before the fingers began to close (75 ms before ft-hands saw the pinch, since the eyes often move on as the hand closes), and a quick tap clicks there. Pinch, hold and move the hand to nudge the pointer: nothing moves until the hand has moved 4 degrees (about 3 cm at arm's length), then the pointer follows at 0.3 times the hand's movement. Letting go clicks where the pointer is, and the correction is a lesson for the gaze tracker, as with the mouse. Held still for `POINTER_GAZE_HOLD` (0.5 s) instead, the pinch is a real press at that point, and the hand drags at `POINTER_GRIP_GAIN`. A middle pinch is a right click the same way. Hold a pinch with one hand and pinch with the other: both buttons are down, left for the first hand and right for the second, each until its own pinch opens (a first pinch not yet pressed becomes a press at once, where the pointer is), as when you hold both of a mouse's buttons. The rules and timings are in `pointer/helper/gazehand.h`.
- **Without gaze mode,** a pinch is a real press, like the mouse's button: pressed when it closes, released when it opens, and while it's held the hand drags the pointer. A tap is still a click where the pointer is.
- **Grip to drag.** Closing the hand presses where the pointer is, the hand moves the pointer, and opening the hand releases. So a title bar moves its window, a panel's grab bar carries the panel, and text gets selected. A panel the hand ray carries by its bar (or a floating window by its title bar) stays upright and facing you wherever the ray takes it (2026-10-13: the ray can't tilt it, so the wrist's turn shouldn't either); a controller's laser still carries it rigidly.
- A pinch ended by losing the hand, or by a grip taking over, doesn't click.
- The hand's movement is taken in the room, from where the eye was when the gesture began, so turning your head doesn't move the pointer. The first gesture while the pointer is off only wakes it. Gestures are ignored in a VR game (unless the dashboard is up), with the headset off, and while the mouse's button is held.

Settings in `~/.config/frametop.conf`:

- `POINTER_HANDS` (0): 1 turns pinches and grips on.
- `POINTER_GAZE_PINCH` (1): in gaze mode, 0 turns clicks at the gaze off (pinches and grips with no ray do nothing), leaving the ray when gaze is off and direct touch. Frametop Input Settings' Hands page has the three hand inputs as switches of their own (`hands/handsmode.py`, `methods_of`).
- `POINTER_PINCH_GAIN` (0.5): without gaze mode, a held pinch moves the pointer this many times the hand's angle, seen from the eye. Under 1 gives precision.
- `POINTER_PINCH_DEADZONE` (1.5): without gaze mode, how many degrees the pinching hand moves before the pointer does, so a tap's jitter and the pinch point shifting as the fingers close don't move it.
- `POINTER_GAZE_HAND_RAY` (0): in gaze mode, 1 keeps the hand ray beside the gaze (a raised, pointing hand's pinches are then ray pinches).
- `POINTER_GAZE_PINCH_DEADZONE` (4): in gaze mode, how many degrees a held pinch moves before it nudges the pointer. A tap's jitter and a hand drifting while you look stay inside it.
- `POINTER_GAZE_PINCH_GAIN` (0.3): in gaze mode, past the dead zone the pointer moves this many times the hand's angle. Low, so a nudge is a fine correction.
- `POINTER_GRIP_GAIN` (1): a grip moves the pointer this many times the hand's angle.
- `POINTER_GRIP_BELOW` (0.35): grips that begin more than this many metres below the eyes are ignored, because hands resting on a desk curl like a loose fist. Pinches have no such limit: deliberate ones sat 0.35-0.45 m below the eyes with the elbow resting.
- `POINTER_PINCH_TYPING` (2), `POINTER_TYPING_RAISE` (0.08 m), `POINTER_TYPING_BELOW` (0.2 m), `POINTER_TYPING_POSTURE` (0.3 s), `POINTER_TYPING_RECENT` (600 s), `POINTER_TYPING_KEEP` (300 s), `POINTER_TYPING_MOUSE` (10 s), `POINTER_DESK_MODEL` (unset), `POINTER_DESK_MIN` (0.8): typing on a real keyboard. For `POINTER_PINCH_TYPING` seconds after a key, no hand pinches, grips, pokes or shows a ray, except one lifted off the keys; a hand that was on the keys stays held back through any pause until it rises `POINTER_TYPING_RAISE` above where it typed (or turns its palm up); both hands down on the keys together count as typing before the first key. A hand on the keys stays held back while the cameras can't see it (looking up at a screen) for up to `POINTER_TYPING_KEEP`, and for `POINTER_TYPING_MOUSE` after the mouse was used a hand that comes down at the desk is held back the same way, keys or not, as is one a trained desk model (`POINTER_DESK_MODEL`) puts on the keyboard or mouse. `pointer/helper/typing.h` and `docs/hands-typing.md` have the rules and the measurements.
- `POINTER_HAND_RAY` (1), `POINTER_RAY_DEADZONE` (2.5 degrees the ray turns during a pinch before the press drags), `POINTER_RAY_PUSH` (1), `POINTER_RAY_SCROLL` (1; -1 turns scrolling round), `POINTER_RAY_RELEASE` (2 s after the ray goes before a pointer it woke lets go), `POINTER_RAY_GRACE` (0.5 s a hand keeps the laser after its ray drops; the other hand's ray takes it after that, or at once by pinching or raising): the hand ray.
- The ray's smoothing (`include/fh_smooth.h`, through `fh_ray.h`): One Euro filters run in continuous time, so the pointer's pose moves at the helper's rate (about 100 Hz) instead of stepping at the tracker's 15-30 Hz; the direction is filtered on the sphere. `POINTER_RAY_SMOOTH_MINCUTOFF` (0.8 Hz with the hand still), `POINTER_RAY_SMOOTH_BETA` (0.06 Hz more per degree a second it turns), `POINTER_RAY_SMOOTH_HOLD` (0.5 Hz still while a pinch is held: a drag's precision). The reach, push and pull's input, has a heavier one of its own. `POINTER_RAY_STEADY` (1): while a pinch is held (and 0.25 s after), the ray pivots on where the shoulder was at the press, then eases back to the body model over 0.5 s; the model's shoulder follows the head, which made looking around move what was dragged. `POINTER_RAY_TAP` (0.3 s) and `POINTER_RAY_TAP_DEADZONE` (6 degrees): a pinch's first 0.3 s takes 6 degrees of turn before it drags, so a quick tap whose hand swung stays a click; after that `POINTER_RAY_DEADZONE` applies. When the press starts dragging the pointer carries on from where it was, no jump. On the probe-moves replay (`pointer/tools/rayeval` on `frame-hands/dumps/probe-moves-v4.dump`, read at 90 Hz) the pose's second difference per tick went from 0.90-0.96 degrees rms (p90 1.4) to 0.24-0.26 (p90 0.24) at about the same lag (110 ms behind the capture, against 105).
- The ray's body and hand models (`include/fh_ray.h`; ideas and formulas from StereoKit, MIT): `POINTER_RAY_TORSO_WRISTS` (1): the shoulders face between the head (counted twice) and the wrists seen, so looking aside while pointing swings the ray less. `POINTER_RAY_HAND_WEIGHT` (0; 0..1): how far the ray turns from the shoulder's line toward the hand's own direction (`POINTER_RAY_HAND_PITCH` -30 and `POINTER_RAY_HAND_YAW` 8 degrees off the palm's axes). `POINTER_RAY_ANCHOR` (0): the knuckle point pinned to the palm while a pinch is held. The last two measured worse on replays (`pointer/tools/rayeval`; numbers in `fh_ray.h`), so they're off.

## Flags

When tracking goes wrong in real use, flag it: `ft-handsctl flag [note]`, or by voice with frame-voice's voice commands (its `commands` setting: "flag that" runs `ft-handsctl flag` with the words after it as the note, e.g. "flag that, fingers crossed"). The offline tools (frame-hands' `train/flags`) relabel what was saved with the teacher, find what went wrong and in which situations, and make guided capture sessions for the weakest ones.

ft-hands keeps a rolling buffer in RAM (`track/flagbuf.h`, `HANDS_FLAG_BUFFER=1`): per processed set, a mono crop around each view the tracker used (its landmark crop's centre, 1.6 times its size, axis aligned, at most `HANDS_FLAG_CROP` px a side), with where it is in the image, so labels made on it lift back to the image and triangulate; a lost view is still cropped where it was for a second, so the teacher can say whether the hand was there; and its hands, gestures and each view's landmarks. It keeps `HANDS_FLAG_SECONDS` and at most `HANDS_FLAG_MB` of crops; nothing is written until a flag. Cost, measured (2026-10-09, a replayed ring with two hands in view): about 80 crops a second at 20 sets a second, 56 MB of crops for 14 s at 224 px (ft-hands' peak RSS 101 -> 159 MB), and 0.1 ms a crop on a tracking core (A720), so about 1% of a core; ft-hands' CPU time was the same with and without it. At 30 sets a second with four views the 80 MB cap can trim the oldest seconds.

`ft-handsctl flag` makes `HANDS_FLAG_DIR/flag-<time>/` and asks ft-hands for the buffer through `run_dir/flag`; ft-hands keeps going for 2 s more, then a thread of its own (off the tracking cores, lowest priority) writes `manifest.json` (written last), `sets.jsonl`, `crops.jsonl` and `crops.bin`. Once ft-hands has taken the request, the drawn hands pulse cyan for half a second (ft-handview's `flash`; `HANDS_FLAG_FLASH=0` turns it off; `HANDS_FLAG_TONE=1` also plays a short tone). It returns then, and in the background saves one frame of your view from `/dev/video99` as `view.jpg` (`HANDS_FLAG_VIEW=0` skips it), the head's poses over the window from ft-handview (`poses`) as `hmd.jsonl`, and `ctl.json`. ft-hands logs each flag ("flag:", "flag written:"), and the status line has the buffer's size and cost. A flag while one is being taken adds its note to it. The format is in frame-hands' `notes/flag-failures.md`. The crops show your hands and some of the room: keep flags with your recordings.

`ft-handreplay REC --flag-at S1,S2 --flag-out DIR` (or `--flag-every S`) makes the same flags from a recording, with the same buffer, for testing the offline tools before real flags exist; `--flag-crop PX`, `--flag-scale F`, `--flag-mb MB` and `--flag-seconds S` change the buffer.
